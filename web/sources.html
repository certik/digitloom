Open data, traceable origins

Meet the word library.

DigitLoom combines pronunciations, word frequencies, and grammatical hints from three open datasets. Its build records immutable source revisions and SHA-256 checksums so the dictionary can be reproduced and inspected.

CMUdict supplies the sounds

The Carnegie Mellon Pronouncing Dictionary is Copyright 1993-2015 Carnegie Mellon University. DigitLoom translates its ARPABET pronunciations into digits. Alternate pronunciations with distinct codes are kept; identical word/code pairs are merged. The source's license and disclaimer are included unchanged.

wordfreq puts familiar words first

Ranking uses the English large frequency list from wordfreq v3.2, Copyright 2022 Robyn Speer. DigitLoom reads frequency bins for exact lowercase spellings, not wordfreq's tokenization or phrase estimation. Frequency ties are alphabetical; words missing from the frequency list remain available at the end of their group.

The data is licensed under Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 International. The full wordfreq attribution notice covers its contributors and source terms.

Source-corpus credit includes Google Books Ngrams and Syntactic Ngrams, the Leeds Internet Corpus, Wikipedia, ParaCrawl, OPUS OpenSubtitles 2018 and the OpenSubtitles project, and the SUBTLEX authors including Marc Brysbaert and colleagues. SUBTLEX is freely available data. Source links and additional conditions are provided in the notice.

WordNet adds grammatical hints

WordNet 3.0 Copyright 2006 by Princeton University. All rights reserved. DigitLoom uses noun, verb, and adjective indexes and exception lists from NLTK's WordNet 3.0 distribution. Inflected forms are matched to indexed lemmas where possible. This is a modified, derived use, not the original WordNet database. Its license and disclaimer remain attached.

What the build changes

The build turns phonemes into digit strings, assigns frequency ranks and possible parts of speech, and groups words by exact code. It does not guess pronunciations from spelling. Stress and most vowels are ignored, but the r-colored vowel ER encodes 4. TH and DH encode 1, NG encodes 2, and ZH encodes 6. Each repeated consonant sound contributes its own digit.

Pronunciations without any encoded digits cannot advance a number and are excluded from suggestions. Source metadata lists these exclusions. A source dictionary can contain errors or omit a pronunciation; its coverage is not a claim to include every English word or accent.

The compact download separates word spellings and part-of-speech masks into columns, orders code groups for compression, and applies gzip. Unpacking restores the complete ranked dictionary and source metadata. Browsers with native decompression use it directly; others load JSON. No externally hosted decoder is needed.
