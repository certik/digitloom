Open data, traceable origins

Meet the word library.

DigitLoom combines pronunciations, word frequencies, and grammatical hints from three open datasets. Its build records immutable source revisions and SHA-256 checksums so the dictionary can be reproduced and inspected.

CMUdict supplies the sounds

The Carnegie Mellon Pronouncing Dictionary is Copyright 1993-2015 Carnegie Mellon University. DigitLoom translates its ARPABET pronunciations into digits. Alternate pronunciations with distinct codes are kept; identical word/code pairs are merged. The source's license and disclaimer are included unchanged.

wordfreq puts familiar words first

Ranking uses the English large frequency list from wordfreq v3.2, Copyright 2022 Robyn Speer. DigitLoom reads frequency bins for exact lowercase spellings, not wordfreq's tokenization or phrase estimation. Frequency ties are alphabetical; words missing from the frequency list remain available at the end of their group. Green highlights default to Zipf 3.5, approximately 3.2 occurrences per million words, without a per-group quota. The word builder's Filter control can adjust this cutoff and hide lower-frequency alternatives. Unranked words are never highlighted. PAO peg ideas use the default cutoff.

Source-corpus credit includes Google Books Ngrams and Syntactic Ngrams, the Leeds Internet Corpus, Wikipedia, ParaCrawl, OPUS OpenSubtitles 2018 and the OpenSubtitles project, and the SUBTLEX authors including Marc Brysbaert and colleagues. SUBTLEX is freely available data. Source links and additional conditions are provided in the notice.

WordNet adds grammatical hints

WordNet 3.0 Copyright 2006 by Princeton University. All rights reserved. DigitLoom uses noun, verb, and adjective indexes and exception lists from NLTK's WordNet 3.0 distribution. Inflected forms are matched to indexed lemmas where possible. This is a modified, derived use, not the original WordNet database. Its license and disclaimer remain attached.

What the build changes

The build turns phonemes into digit strings, assigns frequency ranks and possible parts of speech, and groups words by exact code. Each record retains its exact wordfreq Zipf score in hundredths, or zero when no frequency is available. It does not guess pronunciations from spelling. Stress and most vowels are ignored, but the r-colored vowel ER encodes 4. TH and DH encode 1, NG encodes 2, and ZH encodes 6. Each repeated consonant sound contributes its own digit.

Pronunciations without any encoded digits cannot advance a number and are excluded from suggestions. Source metadata lists these exclusions. A source dictionary can contain errors or omit a pronunciation; its coverage is not a claim to include every English word or accent.

The compact download separates word spellings, part-of-speech masks, and frequency values into columns, orders code groups for compression, and applies gzip. Unpacking restores the complete ranked dictionary, frequency scores, and source metadata. Browsers with native decompression use it directly; others load JSON. No externally hosted decoder is needed.

The starter PAO table

The PAO page opens an editable starter table, the DigitLoom starter PAO, compiled by DigitLoom contributors. Its 110 rows combine assembled examples for pairs 00 to 99 with single-digit associations supplied in contributor feedback, including their names and context cues as provided. It is not anyone's complete personal table. Its pegs are words from the CMUdict-derived dictionary described above. The starter is distributed under CC BY-SA 4.0, separately from the MIT-licensed software. The file records its license and attribution, and exports of tables edited from it keep both fields. Credit DigitLoom contributors, link to this page, indicate your changes, and share adaptations under the same terms.

The 110-row starter changes three generated pair assignments to avoid duplicates with the supplied singles: person 14 becomes woodpecker, action 37 becomes brews, and action 99 becomes gnaws. Existing personal imports retain their own assignments; version-1 files gain blank single-digit rows.

Tables you import keep the license and attribution they contain; DigitLoom displays them as plain text and adds no claims of its own. A new blank table starts with neither. PAO associations are never merged into the word library, and your tables stay in the open tab unless you export them.
